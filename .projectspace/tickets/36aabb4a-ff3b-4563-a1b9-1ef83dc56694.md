# Homepage: hero, workflow, feature sections, and FAQ

## Intended outcome
A new visitor can tell from `/` alone what Knightbook is, what it does and does not do, and how to download it.

## Acceptance criteria
- The hero uses the spec §4 copy (or an edit the user approves), with the primary download action and a secondary "Read the getting-started guide" link.
- The download action reads the release-data file: "Downloads are not available yet" when there is no release, a preview label beside it for a preview, and an "All downloads" link that is always present. It never points at a missing asset, source archive or CI artifact.
- A four-step workflow section (open, keep context together, work with your AI tool, verify and decide) links to the full guide.
- Three feature sections (files you keep using, a shared view of the work, your completion decision), with the one-sentence MCP explanation near the AI section.
- The FAQ answers the eight spec §4 questions within their stated boundaries. The license and pricing answer comes from the license decision ticket.
- The page ends with the same download action and a getting-started link.
- A test checks the rendered page against a list of banned claims ("works with every AI", "fully offline AI", "secure by default", "zero data leaves your device", "open source" unless the license says so), with no testimonials or usage counts.
- Places waiting on screenshots or decisions are marked as drafts that fail a production build.
- On wide screens a concise hero sits beside a large board image; on narrow screens copy, actions and image stack in that order.

## Notes
Spec §2 is the list of safe claims and their limits. Check every claim against the release being offered, not the specification. No client logos until a client guide is tested and brand rules are checked.
